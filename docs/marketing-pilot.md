## Seven X drafts

Each is a single post under 280 characters. Media is optional; do not mention an attachment unless it exists.

### Day 1 — Introduce the product

I built ARC: three daily quests for personal growth, with XP, levels and streaks. It's a small free beta, and I'm looking for people who enjoy game-style progress. Would this help you get started, or become another app you forget?

### Day 2 — Test the problem

What gets in the way of self-improvement for you: choosing what to do, starting, or coming back tomorrow? I'm building ARC around small daily quests, and I want to understand where the friction really is.

### Day 3 — Explain the loop

ARC's loop is simple: open today's three quests, choose one, do it, and mark it complete to earn XP. The action happens outside the app. What would make that loop worth returning to tomorrow?

### Day 4 — Invite testers

ARC is live: https://www.tryarc.co.in

Three daily quests, XP, levels and streaks. It's a free five-person beta with limited spaces. Interested in trying it for a week and giving candid feedback? Reply here and I'll check availability.

### Day 5 — Show a real quest

One ARC quest: learn a small concept for ten minutes, close the source, and write three things you remember. That's the kind of practical action the app rewards with XP. Which feels more motivating: visible progress or a streak?

### Day 6 — Ask about the design

ARC currently gives you three daily quests across five attributes: Strength, Intelligence, Vitality, Charisma and Perception. Would that feel focused, or would you prefer to work on just one area? I'm testing the idea with a small beta.

### Day 7 — Invite a useful conversation

I'm building ARC for people who like game progression and want a clearer next step for personal growth. If you've tried habit apps, what made you stop using them? I'd like to understand that before adding more features.

If actual feedback has arrived by day seven, replace the last draft with a specific, consented finding. Never write an outcome in advance. If beta capacity is full, replace recruitment language with an interest request; do not promise a working waitlist until one exists.

## Fourteen additional X drafts

Use one morning and one afternoon draft per day alongside the existing evening draft. Choose media only when real assets exist. Keep beta claims synchronized with the deployed capacity, and link to the waitlist only after it is live.

Day 1 morning: A small learning task for today: spend ten minutes on one concept, close the source, and write down what you remember. What would you choose to learn?

Day 1 afternoon: ARC is a web app: email-code sign-in, three daily quests, and XP for completed actions. The aim is to make the next step clear. What would you need to see before giving it a try?

Day 2 morning: Try turning "be more productive" into one specific action: choose a manageable task, set aside optional distractions, and work on it for ten minutes. Then write your next step.

Day 2 afternoon: In ARC, one completed quest makes an active day; finishing all three makes a perfect day. Would keeping a streak with one action encourage you, or would streaks add pressure?

Day 3 morning: A practical task can be preparation, too: put one distraction away and choose a realistic time to wind down tonight. Progress doesn't always have to mean doing more.

Day 3 afternoon: ARC currently has authored quests rather than AI-generated tasks. I want to learn which instructions people actually find useful before expanding the catalog. What makes a daily task feel worthwhile to you?

Day 4 morning: What makes you abandon a habit app first: setup, notifications, repetitive tasks, or losing a streak? I'm trying to understand the moment when a tool stops helping.

Day 4 afternoon: ARC tracks progress across five attributes, but the actions happen outside the app. Reading, focused work and practical preparation can all be quests. Which area would you want to work on first?

Day 5 morning: A learning exercise: explain a small idea aloud in two minutes. Notice where you lose the thread, then make the explanation simpler. What topic could you try that with?

Day 5 afternoon: XP in ARC is a record of completed quests, not a measurement of someone's worth or abilities. Does that distinction make game-style progress more useful to you?

Day 6 morning: Is a daily reminder helpful, or does it become one more notification to ignore? I'm interested in what actually gets people back to a practice they chose.

Day 6 afternoon: ARC gives users a focus and a daily set of quests. During this beta, that focus stays fixed. Would that help you stay with one direction, or feel too restrictive?

Day 7 morning: If a personal-growth app disappeared tomorrow, what would you miss: its prompts, your history, visible progress, or nothing? That feels like a more useful question than whether an app looks nice.

Day 7 afternoon: ARC's beta is an experiment in small daily actions and game-style progression. I'd rather learn what feels repetitive or confusing than add features blindly. What would you test first?

## Twenty-second demo script

0–4 seconds: Show the actual Today screen. Voice: "What if getting started meant choosing one of three daily quests?"

4–10 seconds: Open a quest's real instructions. Voice: "ARC gives you practical actions for personal growth."

10–16 seconds: Show your own account's genuine completion and resulting XP. Voice: "Do the action, mark it complete, and see your progress."

16–20 seconds: Show the site address. Voice: "I'm testing a small free beta at tryarc.co.in. Tell me if you'd like to try it."

Do not award a completion merely to manufacture footage. Use a genuinely completed quest or label any demo footage clearly. Hide email addresses and private account information.

## Tracking and decisions

Keep one row per published post in marketing-results.csv. Record URL, platform, message, media and 24-hour results; missing data remains blank, never zero by assumption. Record interested testers and confirmed onboarding separately. Ask consenting testers how they found ARC; existing analytics do not establish channel attribution. No new tracking code is installed by this campaign.

Primary outcome: remaining spaces filled by interested testers who actually use the app. Secondary outcomes: relevant replies, profile visits and expressed interest. Impressions and likes measure reach, not product usefulness. Seven days and four testers cannot establish product-market fit or prove a channel wins.

On day four, compare which messages sparked useful conversations and adapt later drafts. On day seven, report actual reach, replies, recruited testers and observed return use, with data gaps. If posts are removed, inspect the stated rule and stop that placement rather than repeatedly resubmitting.
